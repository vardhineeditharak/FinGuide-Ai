# Understanding Interest Rates and Loans

## Key terms
- **Principal**: The original amount borrowed or invested.
- **Interest rate**: The cost of borrowing money, expressed as a % per year (per annum).
- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate)**: Total yearly cost of a loan including fees, not just the base interest rate — always compare APR, not just the advertised rate.
- **Simple interest**: Interest calculated only on the principal.
- **Compound interest**: Interest calculated on principal + previously accumulated interest — grows faster over time.
- **Reducing balance vs flat rate**: Reducing balance interest is charged only on the outstanding amount (cheaper for the borrower); flat rate is charged on the full original principal for the whole tenure (more expensive, even if the advertised rate looks similar or lower).

## What is a "safe" or reasonable interest rate? (general educational guidance, not financial advice)
As of recent RBI trends, indicative ranges seen in the formal banking sector in India are roughly:
- Home loans: ~8–10% per annum
- Personal loans (banks/NBFCs): ~10–16% per annum
- Credit card outstanding: ~36–48% per annum (very high — avoid carrying a balance)
- Gold loans: ~9–15% per annum

Rates above these ranges, especially from unregistered lenders or apps, should be treated as a red flag. Always check whether the lender is an RBI-registered NBFC or bank — you can verify NBFC registration on the RBI website.

## Things to check before taking any loan
- Confirm the lender is registered with RBI (banks, NBFCs) — verify on rbi.org.in.
- Ask for the total cost (APR) including processing fees, not just the "interest rate."
- Understand prepayment/foreclosure charges.
- Check whether interest is calculated on reducing balance or flat rate.
- Read the loan agreement fully before signing; do not rely on verbal promises.
- Avoid loan apps that ask for excessive permissions (contacts, gallery, camera) on your phone.

## Basic budgeting principle (50-30-20 rule)
A common educational framework for managing personal income:
- 50% of income → needs (rent, groceries, utilities, EMIs)
- 30% of income → wants (entertainment, dining out)
- 20% of income → savings and investments

This is a general guideline and should be adapted to individual circumstances.
