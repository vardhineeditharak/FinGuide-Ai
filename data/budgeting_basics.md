# Personal Budgeting and Money Management Basics

## Why budget?
A budget helps you track income vs expenses so you can save consistently, avoid debt, and plan for future goals (education, home, retirement, emergencies).

## Steps to create a simple monthly budget
1. **List all income** — salary, freelance income, any other regular inflow.
2. **List all expenses** — fixed (rent, EMI, insurance) and variable (groceries, travel, entertainment).
3. **Categorize** using a framework like the 50-30-20 rule (needs/wants/savings).
4. **Track daily/weekly** using a notebook, spreadsheet, or budgeting app.
5. **Review monthly** and adjust categories where overspending happens.

## Building an emergency fund
- Aim for 3–6 months of essential expenses saved in a liquid, easily accessible account (savings account or liquid mutual fund).
- Build it gradually — even small consistent amounts (e.g., 5–10% of income) add up over time.

## Good financial habits
- Pay credit card bills in full each month to avoid high interest charges (often 36–48% p.a.).
- Automate savings — set up auto-transfers to a savings/investment account right after salary credit.
- Avoid lifestyle inflation — try to save a portion of any salary increase rather than increasing spending proportionally.
- Diversify savings across instruments based on goals and time horizon (e.g., short-term: savings account/FD; long-term: mutual funds/PPF, based on individual risk appetite).
- Track your credit score (CIBIL) periodically — a good score (750+) helps you get loans at better rates.

## Digital tools that can help
- Bank apps and UPI apps often have built-in expense trackers.
- Government platforms like the National Centre for Financial Education (NCFE) offer free financial literacy resources.
